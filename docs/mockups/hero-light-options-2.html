Quieter, more premium stage light

Six subtler takes. Every one moves on a cycle of 20 seconds or more, uses at most a handful of moving parts, never pulses on the beat, and keeps the headline the brightest thing on screen. Some you'll only notice if you watch for a while; that's the idea. Today's hero and your round-one pick, Haze & dust, are first, for comparison. DARK checks both themes and STILL freezes everything.

ROUND TWO

OPTION 1

Single key light

Haze & dust, distilled
WHAT IT DOES
One warm key light falls from just off frame, top left, across the headline. Its angle shifts a couple of degrees over about forty seconds. A few dozen motes drift slowly, and you only see them inside the beam.
WHY IT'S CALMER
It keeps what you liked about Haze & dust (real light falling through a room) with one source instead of two. It's warm white rather than red and brass, and there's no lamp in shot.
COST
One layer turning, one small canvas of about 40 motes at 30 fps, asleep off screen.
OPTION 2

Light leak

warm film light at the edges
WHAT IT DOES
Warm light bleeds in from the top-left corner, like a light leak on film, with a faint red answer from the bottom right. Over about half a minute they slowly trade strength, and a pale brass glow drifts across the top.
WHY IT'S PREMIUM
It's photographic, not theatrical. Nothing has an edge or a shape to track, so the page just feels warm and alive. The most restrained option.
COST
Three layers fading and drifting. The cheapest option.
OPTION 3

Louvre light

late light through stage louvres
WHAT IT DOES
Soft bars of warm light fall across the upper right, the way light comes through louvres or blinds. They lean and drift a few pixels a second, and fade out towards the edges.
WHY IT'S PREMIUM
It's editorial: the look of a magazine shoot or a quiet studio before a session. It gives the page a sense of time of day without asking for attention.
COST
One layer that turns and shifts slowly.
OPTION 4

Halftone glow

light that only exists in the print
WHAT IT DOES
No beams at all. A soft light wanders slowly across the stage, and the halftone dots it passes over swell and warm to brass, as if the print itself were lit. The dots stay locked to the page's grid as the light moves.
WHY IT'S PREMIUM
It's the most "Palm/Mute" option: it uses the hero's own print texture instead of adding lighting on top. Subtle, crafted and unique to the brand.
COST
Two layers moving in opposite directions. No canvas.
OPTION 5

Footlights

the glow from the lip of the stage
WHAT IT DOES
A low warm glow rises from the bottom edge, as from footlights along the front of a stage, with five soft lamps along the lip. Each breathes on its own slow cycle (9 to 14 seconds), so the light never quite repeats.
WHY IT'S PREMIUM
It grounds the headline: the type seems to stand on a stage rather than float on paper. The top of the hero stays clean.
COST
Six layers fading, no movement at all.
OPTION 6

Sheen

light passing over gloss, now and then
WHAT IT DOES
The stage rests under a faint warm glow. Every twenty seconds a soft band of light glides across it once, over about four seconds, like light catching a glossy record sleeve, then everything is still again.
WHY IT'S PREMIUM
Stillness most of the time makes the one moment of movement feel deliberate. It's the closest to a luxury-product page.
COST
Two layers sliding by transform, idle 80% of the time.
