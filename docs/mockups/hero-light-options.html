OPTION 1

Haze & dust

like a club before doors open
WHAT IT DOES
The two beams get a hot white core inside a wider coloured cone, with a visible source at the top. Slow banks of haze drift through, and dust motes float everywhere but only light up while a beam passes over them.
WHY IT FEELS PREMIUM
Depth. The light has a volume to fall through, so it reads as real light in a room, not a gradient on paper. It stays closest to today's look.
COST
Two layers turning and one small canvas of about 90 motes at 30 fps, asleep off screen.
OPTION 2

Moving-head rig

a light show on the beat
WHAT IT DOES
A thin truss runs along the top with four moving heads. Every bar at 180 BPM (1.33s) they snap to a new look: a fan, crossed beams, all on the headline, a chase. Each beam leaves a soft pool of light where it lands.
WHY IT FEELS PREMIUM
It's choreographed, not random, and it's in time with the "LIVE · 180 BPM" tag. It reads as a real show and ties the hero to the music.
COST
Eight layers moving by transitions, one timer a bar. The busiest option. It could calm to every 4 bars if it's too much.
OPTION 3

Gobo projection

a theatre texture, slowly turning
WHAT IT DOES
One warm key light projects a break-up gobo of brass and red slivers with fine rings, through a soft iris. The pattern turns once every minute or so and wanders slowly across the stage, with a vignette around the edges.
WHY IT FEELS PREMIUM
Restraint. It's slow, textural and editorial, like a theatre before the curtain. It's the quietest option and keeps the headline the hero.
COST
Three layers, rotation and translation only.
OPTION 4

Backlight & rays

the band walks on
WHAT IT DOES
A warm bloom sits behind the headline, the way a backlight sits behind a band. Two sets of god rays turn very slowly in opposite directions, and the halftone clears around the light. The bloom breathes once every four bars at 180 BPM.
WHY IT FEELS PREMIUM
It's cinematic. The headline seems to stand in front of the light, which gives the type real presence. One focal point, and calm.
COST
Three layers: two rotating, one breathing.
OPTION 5

Follow spot

the show starts when you arrive
WHAT IT DOES
The stage starts dimmed. On arrival a follow spot irises open, a crisp oval with a thin red rim. It then follows your pointer with a weighted, springy lag, like a spot operator tracking you. The halftone turns red inside the beam. On touch screens it glides between the headline and the button.
WHY IT FEELS PREMIUM
It's interactive and personal: the light is on you. The iris-in makes arriving feel like an event. It's today's spotlight idea, done properly.
COST
One layer moved by transform and one mask that follows it, updated only while moving.
OPTION 6

Anamorphic flare

shot on a cinema lens
WHAT IT DOES
A light sits just off frame, top right. It throws a long horizontal anamorphic streak across the stage and a line of lens ghosts. Out-of-focus bokeh from the rest of the rig drifts behind the headline and shifts gently with your pointer. The source flickers very slightly, like a real lamp.
WHY IT FEELS PREMIUM
It borrows the language of film and music-video photography. It's warm and glossy, with a lot of depth for very little movement.
COST
About 13 small layers floating by transform, plus one parallax layer.
